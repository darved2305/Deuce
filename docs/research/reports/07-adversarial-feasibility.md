# Track 07: red-team report

**Verdict:** A 24-hour team should not commit to reliable six-sport analysis, fully offline phone inference, and two polished products plus a complete developer platform. The evidence supports a narrower demo: a phone-first gallery workflow, one or two explicitly supported conditions, visible uncertainty, and results tied to source frames. Cross-sport capability, mobile speed, and accuracy on arbitrary phone footage remain **UNVERIFIED**.

Prices below are public US prices checked on 2026-10-10. India pricing may differ. Paper numbers are published results, not measurements of your build.

## 1. Where one-camera ball and shuttle tracking fails

| Case | Verified evidence | What it means for this demo |
|---|---|---|
| **Badminton: shuttle size, speed, and lighting** | A deployed badminton line-review system used cameras around the court. At 100 fps, it measured shuttle travel of **70–330 mm per frame**, roughly ten cork diameters; the authors found **150–200 fps** a good real-time compromise, leaving **5–6 ms per frame** for processing. Of nine surveyed venues, only one had flicker-free lights. Their cameras were 800×600 and capable of 200 fps. [Practical deployment study](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22218098) | A 30 fps phone clip is poor evidence for precise landing calls. This study describes one deployed system, not every venue, but it shows why shuttle visibility, lighting flicker, and high frame rate matter. |
| **Table tennis: 30 fps capture sensitivity** | In one 720×480 experiment, a color-based detector reported **22.5% detection accuracy at 30 fps**, **93.2% at 60 fps**, and **93.6% at 120 fps**. Its processing rate was 45.3 fps. This is one method and setup, not a prediction for every model. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10673905/) | Fast processing cannot recover detail absent from the source video. A 30 fps demo can miss or blur a ball even when inference is fast. |
| **Table tennis: high-speed benchmark conditions** | TTNet’s OpenTTGames data uses **1920×1080 at 120 fps**. The paper reports **97.5% ball-detection accuracy**, **2 px RMSE**, and **97% event-spotting accuracy** on its test data. It reports under **6 ms per input tensor on a consumer GPU**, not on a phone. [TTNet paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09927) | These results do not validate 30 fps phone footage or mobile speed. |
| **Table tennis: blur spans large distances** | BlurBall describes table tennis balls reaching **35 m/s** and adds labels for the center and orientation of the blur streak. At that speed, the ball travels about **1.17 m between frames at 30 fps**. The 1.17 m is a calculation, not a measured phone failure rate. [BlurBall paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/CVsports/papers/Gossard_BlurBall_Joint_Ball_and_Motion_Blur_Estimation_for_Table_Tennis_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf) | A single sharp point may not exist in a frame. The model may need to infer from blur and neighboring frames. |
| **Tennis: headline accuracy falls across videos** | Original TrackNet reports **99.7% precision and 97.3% recall** on one match. With nine additional videos and 10-fold cross-validation, it reports **75.7% recall and 84.3% F1**. The paper describes the ball as small, blurry, sometimes afterimaged, and sometimes invisible. [TrackNet paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03698) | Don’t quote the single-match result as broad reliability. Show cross-video results and label the test conditions. |
| **Camera angle and framing** | MonoTrack describes shuttle occlusion and frequent out-of-frame motion. Its main dataset contains static, overhead broadcast views from behind the court. The paper limits its end-to-end work to badminton singles and fixed-camera, approximately broadcast-view footage. [MonoTrack paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/CVSports/papers/Liu_MonoTrack_Shuttle_Trajectory_Reconstruction_From_Monocular_Badminton_Video_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf) | A low phone angle or arbitrary side view is outside that tested distribution. I found no primary source establishing a universal failure angle; any cutoff such as “below X degrees” is **UNVERIFIED**. |
| **Moving camera and background assumptions** | RacketVision’s strongest ball-tracking setup uses four frames plus a median background image. The paper reports that background modeling reduced mean distance error by about **54–61%** on its three sports. [RacketVision paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/37362/41324), [official code](https://github.com/OrcustD/RacketVision) | **Inference:** panning or handheld footage weakens a static-background input prior. Tapping court corners helps calibration; it does not remove blur, occlusion, or a moving view. |

## 2. “No training” does not mean sport-neutral

Using someone else’s frozen weights is allowed under your interpretation. It does not prove zero-shot performance on a sport those weights never saw.

| Model or dataset | Verified training or evaluation scope | Deployment facts and limits |
|---|---|---|
| **YOLO11n-seg with COCO weights** | Ultralytics’ COCO class list includes `sports ball` and `tennis racket`, but no dedicated shuttlecock, pickleball, padel-ball, or squash-ball labels. [COCO class list](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/blob/main/ultralytics/cfg/datasets/coco.yaml) | Useful for people and broad object priors. It is not evidence of reliable tiny-ball trajectory tracking. |
| **TrackNet** | The cited original work is trained and tested on tennis broadcasts. Its cross-video recall drop is listed above. [TrackNet paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03698) | Tennis-specific evidence does not transfer automatically to the other sports. |
| **WASB** | WASB is a **1.5 million parameter** baseline evaluated on five sport datasets: soccer, tennis, badminton, volleyball, and basketball. That does not cover table tennis, pickleball, padel, or squash. [Paper](https://papers.bmvc2023.org/0310.pdf), [official repo](https://github.com/nttcom/WASB-SBDT) | The repo documents Python 3.8, CUDA 11.3, Ubuntu 18.04, and testing on four V100 GPUs. That is a research setup, not a mobile benchmark. Exact weight size and phone speed are **UNVERIFIED**. |
| **RacketVision MS-TrackNetV3** | The 2026 AAAI benchmark covers badminton, table tennis, and tennis. It sources 942 professional broadcasts, forms 1,672 active-play clips, and manually labels 20% of frames. Its best multi-sport model is trained on all three sports. Reported mAP is **71.1** for table tennis, **81.9** for tennis, and **83.1** for badminton; its precision, recall, and localization error vary by sport. [AAAI paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/37362/41324), [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/linfeng302/RacketVision) | This is meaningful cross-sport evidence, but it is training on all three tested sports and benchmark footage, not zero-shot transfer to arbitrary phone videos. The published benchmark does not report phone inference speed. |

A concrete candidate is RacketVision’s `balltrack_best.pth`, listed at **46.6 MB**. Its reference setup documents **Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.1.2, torchvision 0.16.2, CUDA 12.1**, plus version ranges for MMEngine, MMDetection, MMPose, MMCV, and OpenCV. This is a CUDA-oriented research stack, not a ready-to-run Core ML or LiteRT mobile bundle. Conversion, sustained phone throughput, and quantized accuracy are **UNVERIFIED**. The Hugging Face page flags the `.pth` checkpoint as unsafe because it contains pickle imports; use only the official artifact, pin its hash, and verify its weight license before redistribution. [Model files](https://huggingface.co/linfeng302/RacketVision-Models/tree/main/checkpoints), [reference environment](https://github.com/OrcustD/RacketVision)

The defensible wording is: **“No venue-specific labels or fine-tuning. We use frozen models pretrained on these named sports, and mark out-of-scope sports as experimental or unsupported.”** A badminton checkpoint used on pickleball is a valid experiment under your rules. Its zero-shot accuracy is **UNVERIFIED**.

## 3. On-device offline analysis: what is and is not verified

**Verified:** Current phones have local inference frameworks, and iOS and Android provide paths for some long-running work. **Not verified:** a converted racket-sport checkpoint that processes full matches offline at an acceptable sustained speed on the team’s actual phones.

- Apple’s `BGContinuedProcessingTask` starts from a foreground user action. The system may queue it, fail to start it under resource constraints, or terminate it; background GPU use needs an entitlement and is not supported on every device. [Apple long-running tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/BackgroundTasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados), [GPU requirement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgcontinuedprocessingtaskrequest/resources/gpu)
- Android 15’s `mediaProcessing` foreground service has a total **six-hour per 24-hour** limit when used under the documented conditions, and the app must stop shortly after the timeout callback. [Android timeout rules](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout)
- Thermal performance changes during long jobs. Android says devices sustain high performance only for a limited period before throttling. One MediaPipe issue reports a Pixel 9 GPU-delegate failure; one ONNX Runtime issue reports different NNAPI and CPU outputs on several older Android devices. These are device-specific issue reports, not general failure rates. [Android thermal guidance](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/adpf/thermal), [MediaPipe issue](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/5954), [ONNX Runtime issue](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/issues/19518)
- Video must be streamed in bounded memory. **Estimated arithmetic:** one 1920×1080 RGBA frame is about 8.3 MB. Buffering every frame would require about **14.9 GB per minute at 30 fps**, before decoder and model memory. Ten minutes would be about 149 GB. Do not materialize the whole video as frames.
- The earlier Match Studio estimate of **0.5 seconds per frame in browser WASM** is **UNVERIFIED** in this review. If it holds and every frame of a 30 fps source is processed, that is 15× slower than real time; a 10-minute video would take 2.5 hours. This is a calculation using that unverified estimate, not a measured benchmark.
- The RacketVision paper reports no FPS. TTNet’s under-6-ms figure is on a consumer GPU. Neither supports a claim about phone speed.

**Practical split:** keep the phone app responsible for gallery import, calibration, playback, player selection, results, and local history. Put analysis behind one interface. Run on the phone only after the chosen model has been converted and benchmarked on named devices, including a sustained run. If that is not proven, use a local laptop inference service over a private local connection for the demo, and label the compute location. That avoids external internet but is **not** phone-only processing.

For a hosted metadata API, free serverless limits are appropriate for lightweight records, not full-match vision. Cloudflare Workers Free lists **100,000 requests/day and 10 ms CPU per invocation**. Supabase Edge Functions Free lists **2 seconds of active CPU per request and 150 seconds wall time**. [Cloudflare limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/), [Supabase limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/troubleshooting/edge-function-cpu-limits) I did not verify a dependable free hosted GPU endpoint for this workload; that availability is **UNVERIFIED**.

## 5. Licensing traps

- **Ultralytics:** its current license page says AGPL-3.0 is for teams willing to open-source the whole project under AGPL. It says private APIs, SaaS, embedded, and proprietary deployments require an Enterprise license if the project is not fully open-sourced; enterprise pricing is quote-only. This is the licensor’s published position, and the exact derivative-work boundary is a legal question. [Ultralytics license](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)
- **Model code, checkpoint, dataset, and video are separate rights questions.** RacketVision’s code repository says MIT, and its Hugging Face dataset card says MIT. The research paper says videos came from professional YouTube broadcasts. Permission to redistribute or publicly demo the underlying broadcast footage is **UNVERIFIED**. [Code license](https://github.com/OrcustD/RacketVision), [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/linfeng302/RacketVision), [paper source description](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/37362/41324)
- **Dataset terms vary.** SportsMOT lists CC BY-NC 4.0, which is noncommercial. Fine-Badminton’s page restricts the video to noncommercial academic research. Do not bundle either into a product without permission. [SportsMOT](https://github.com/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT/blob/main/README.md), [Fine-Badminton](https://zenodo.org/records/20292976)
- **Public on YouTube is not permission to download and redistribute.** YouTube says Standard is the default license and that it cannot grant rights to another creator’s content. Prefer public-domain, explicit CC BY footage, or written permission; keep attribution and source details. [YouTube license help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468), [Creative Commons BY 4.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/deed.en)

## 6. How judges can detect a fake or overclaimed demo

Expect questions like:

1. “Can you choose this local gallery file and show results computed from these bytes?”
2. “What was computed now, and what was precomputed?”
3. “Can you run it with internet disabled?”
4. “Show the exact model file, runtime, device, OS, and accelerator.”
5. “Open the source frame and timestamp for that claimed event.”
6. “What does the system do when the ball is hidden or out of frame?”
7. “Which held-out video and annotations support this accuracy claim?”
8. “Can you rerun the same input and reproduce the event JSON?”
9. “How does performance change after several minutes?”
10. “Which sports and camera views do you mark unsupported?”

Build answers into the demo. Show the selected video, frame count, live stage timers, processed-frame rate, input SHA-256, model SHA-256, code commit, runtime versions, device, and run start/end. Link each event to source frames and show confidence, visibility, and coverage. Include a held-out clip with manual ground truth. Run the judge-provided clip live when possible, and let the model abstain when it loses the target. A hash proves artifact identity; it does not prove correctness.

## 7. What four people can plausibly ship in 24 hours

**Estimate:** if the mobile shell and model start cleanly, a team of four strong programmers using coding agents can likely build a polished gallery-to-analysis slice, a narrow inference path, player selection, a useful replay surface, and a thin REST/SDK/MCP layer over one result schema.

Likely to look half-done if pursued together: reliable score reconstruction across six sports, accurate speed and line calls, cross-device full-match offline processing, Android and iOS delegate support, and production-quality API/SDK/MCP documentation. LLM-assisted coding reduces implementation time; it does not supply labeled test cases, clean rights, accurate ground truth, sustained mobile throughput, or judge-proof validation.

Spend the depth on one complete interaction: import a permitted clip, configure the court, inspect one or two rallies, see tracking confidence and failure spans, confirm player identity, and query the same saved match through the API or MCP. Put citations from output metrics directly back to timestamps and frames. A six-sport picker with untested metrics will look weaker.

## Claims we must not make

- “It works out of the box across all six sports.”
- “A frozen badminton model works for pickleball,” or that zero-shot transfer is accurate.
- “No training” without clarifying that the frozen model was previously trained on named sports.
- “Fully offline on any phone” or “full matches finish in X minutes” without a sustained, named-device benchmark.
- “Real-time” based on a short, cold run or on desktop GPU speeds.
- “Accurate speed, 3D trajectory, or line calls” from a low-frame-rate, uncalibrated single view.
- “Any angle, venue, lighting, and camera motion.”
- “MIT dataset” or “public YouTube video” as proof that broadcast footage can be redistributed.
- “The hash proves the result is correct.”
- “API, SDK, and MCP make the product novel.”
- “Free hosted inference” without a currently verified endpoint, terms, and compute limit.

## Recommendation

Build one phone-first vertical slice around **a single licensed clip and one tested sport**. The phone should import, display, calibrate, and preserve the match. Show the model’s source-linked results, confidence, and failure spans. If local inference on the chosen phone is not proven early, run inference on a local laptop service and show that the demo needs no external internet while clearly identifying where compute ran. Treat the other sports as experimental or unsupported.

Use one canonical event record for the REST endpoint, TypeScript SDK, and MCP server. Make player identity user-confirmed. Put time into correct replay and visible evidence before court booking, social discovery, six rule engines, or claims of professional officiating.

## Three biggest risks

1. **Capture and domain gap:** fast, tiny targets, blur, low angles, occlusion, and indoor lighting can invalidate ball statistics.
2. **Mobile execution:** sustained offline inference, conversion, thermal behavior, OS interruptions, and Android accelerator compatibility are unverified.
3. **Demo credibility:** unclear footage rights, weak holdout evidence, or precomputed-looking results will undermine otherwise good engineering.

## Open questions only the team can answer

- Which sport can you support with both a legally usable clip and a teammate or coach who can verify the output?
- Which exact iPhone and Android devices will be available at the event?
- Is local laptop inference acceptable if the phone app works without external internet, or is phone-only computation mandatory?
- Does the judging rubric reward verified CV depth more than player-management features and the developer platform?
