# Verdict

**Elevate has no defensible moat yet.** The strongest path is to make a club or tournament’s match records useful to players: each statistic links to video evidence, uncertainty is visible, and corrections are kept. Repeated, consented matches could then build a moment library for coaching and opponent review. That loop has not been proven by the hackathon team yet.

**Do not claim that APIs, MCP, multi-sport configuration, India-first positioning, or full offline analysis are moats on their own.** PB Vision already offers an API, SDK and MCP; PlaySight and Stupa cover several racket sports; and Indian products already serve venues, academies and players. Full post-game analysis offline on a mid-range Android phone remains **UNVERIFIED**.

For most products, the current model version is **UNVERIFIED** because the vendor does not publish it. Company-reported usage and feature claims are identified as such.

## Which moat candidates can hold up?

| Rank | Candidate | What could become hard to copy | Honest weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Consented data network effect and moment library** | Repeated match records linked to players, events, outcomes, evidence frames and accepted corrections could improve history, retrieval and recommendations. Strava is a successful sports example of community history and segments; it reports more than 195 million athletes. ([About Strava](https://press.strava.com/about), [Segment leaderboards](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401900-how-do-i-view-my-segment-results-on-strava)) | Raw video does not compound by itself. You need recurring matches, identity links, rights to use clips, and reviewed corrections. This does **not** mean match data can train a venue- or sport-specific model under your stated no-training rule. First-match value must stand on pretrained models and configuration. |
| **2** | **Academy, club and tournament distribution** | One organizer’s workflow can bring in many matches, repeated players and future reviews. This is the strongest way to seed the data loop. | It is execution, not an existing moat. Playo, Hudle, KheloMore, Stupa and tournament platforms already own parts of these workflows. Elevate has no distribution advantage until a venue or organizer uses it repeatedly. |
| **3** | **Evidence and trust layer** | Stats that open their source frames, expose confidence, abstain when unclear and preserve correction history could earn trust with coaches and organizers. | Evidence UI is copyable. Stupa already advertises frame-accurate review in officiating. Elevate’s distinction would be putting provenance and uncertainty on every consumer insight, then building a trusted correction record. |
| **4** | **India-first Android and local workflow** | Mid-range Android support, Marathi/Hindi UX, rupee pricing and academy/tournament fit could make adoption easier. | Indian companies already target local players and academies. These choices are valuable for distribution, but can be copied. Do not call the product “first in India.” |
| **5** | **Offline, on-device and privacy-first** | Reliable local capture, review and later sync can be valuable at courts with weak connectivity. Baseline shows that offline operation is possible with dedicated tennis hardware. | Full post-game analysis on the team’s target Android phone is **UNVERIFIED**. Offline capture and queued upload are not the same as offline inference. Privacy depends on consent, retention and sharing controls, not a local-processing claim alone. |
| **6** | **Open match format, SDK and MCP** | An exportable record could let other apps, academies and organizers preserve history and build on the analysis. Strava and Garmin FIT show how widely used activity formats can support an ecosystem. FIT is for sport/fitness device data, not a frame-linked racket match record. The ITF’s tennis exchange standard, TODS, is still in development. ([FIT protocol](https://developer.garmin.com/fit/protocol/), [ITF TODS](https://itftennis.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/TODS/overview?homepageId=535920732)) | API/MCP is already offered by PB Vision, and Smashpoint offers MCP. A schema does not create a moat until independent parties use it. A portable JSON match record is a sensible product choice, not a launch-day moat. |
| **7** | **Sport-agnostic configuration packs** | Court geometry, rules and event vocabulary can make it cheaper to add scorekeeping and normalize records across sports. | Config files are easy to copy and do not solve ball or shuttle perception. Stupa and PlaySight already cover several racket sports. New-sport support still needs measured vision performance, not just a rules pack. |

### How to show the strongest claims in the demo

| Claim | Demo sequence | Keep the claim honest |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence and trust | Tap a stat, open the exact supporting clip frames, show confidence, correct one event and show the affected summary update. Make one uncertain event abstain. | Call it verifiable analysis or a review assistant. Do not claim professional officiating accuracy from informal footage. |
| Data loop | Show a match becoming a player record, then retrieve a useful moment from an earlier consented clip beside the current action. | If you seed two clips for the demo, label them as the team’s demo library. Do not imply a live community network effect or model learning from those clips. |
| India and weak connectivity | On the target Android phone, switch to airplane mode, save a match, reopen its cached record and show the sync queue. | Claim offline capture and review only if tested. Show full offline inference only after measuring it on the target phone. |

## Recommendation

Lead with **evidence-linked match analysis** and the **venue-to-player data loop**. Treat India-first Android distribution as the wedge and offline-safe capture as a proof point only after a target-device test. Put an open export/API behind the demo as useful infrastructure, not as the headline moat.

**Two-sentence moat statement:**  
“We are building a verifiable match record from ordinary-phone video: each insight links to its supporting frames and shows confidence, uncertainty and corrections. Academy and tournament workflows can build a consented, portable moment library that makes each player’s next review more useful, while Android-first capture and sync target India’s low-connectivity courts.”

## Three biggest risks

1. **The data loop never starts.** Without recurring venue or tournament use, consented matches and coach/player corrections, the “network effect” is just a feature idea.
2. **Video quality and phone limits break the promise.** Small fast balls or shuttles, low resolution, camera movement, heat, battery and storage can make analysis unreliable. Stupa’s tournament officiating positioning and Baseline’s dedicated-camera approach underline that offline operation and pro-grade tracking are not automatically phone problems solved by software. ([Stupa](https://stupasports.ai/broadcasters), [Baseline offline operation](https://baselinevisionhelpcenter.gorgias.help/en-US/how-the-baseline-camera-works-7452662))
3. **A wrong or exposed record destroys trust.** False calls, unclear corrections, or sharing clips of players without consent could damage the academy/referee channel. Opponent scouting and minors make consent, visibility and deletion rules part of the product.

## Open questions only the team can answer

- Which one sport and backup sport have the best real clips and venue access for the demo?
- Which exact Android phone is the reference device, and what post-game latency and storage limit will you accept?
- Who can provide a repeatable academy or tournament workflow to seed the first match library?
- Will players opt into a shared moment library and opponent scouting, and who can review disputed events?
