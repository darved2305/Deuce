# Track 04: Training-free analysis methods and reliability

**Verdict:** Build an assisted, post-game analyzer for fixed, full-court phone video. Do not promise professional tracking, automatic officiating, or reliable 3D speed across all six racket sports. Court-normalized player movement and user-confirmed scoring are the strongest shared capabilities. Ball tracking, shot labels, line calls, and automatic score should be conditional and allowed to abstain.

This review uses sources available on **10 October 2026**. The reliability tiers below are engineering estimates for amateur phone footage, not measured cross-sport accuracy claims. I found no public benchmark that establishes performance across arbitrary amateur phone videos for all six sports.

## Reliability tiers and capture contract

- **A:** Useful when the capture conditions and setup are met. Not an official ruling.
- **B:** Useful candidate result. Show evidence and allow correction.
- **C:** Experimental. Label it as uncertain and do not use it for official scoring.
- **X:** No compatible public model or benchmark was verified for this sport and feature.

For the best results, ask for a stable, landscape view that includes the whole playing area and both players. Preserve the original frame timestamps and audio. Prefer 1080p and 60 fps; accept 30 fps for player movement, but expect many fast ball events to be missed. Let users tap the court boundary or table corners when automatic calibration is uncertain. These are capture and calibration assumptions, not requirements for collecting training data.

## Court templates

Templates provide metric geometry after the court or table is registered. They do not make a partial or side-on view well-conditioned.

| Sport | Template dimensions and relevant geometry |
|---|---|

| Badminton | 13.40 m long; 5.18 m wide for singles and 6.10 m for doubles. The BWF law PDF also specifies court markings. [BWF Laws of Badminton](https://extranet.bwf.sport/docs/document-system/81/1466/1470/Section%204.1-%20Laws%20of%20Badminton-%2026%20April%202025%20V5.0%20(2).pdf) |

| Padel | 10 m × 20 m. The enclosure and glass/fence walls affect rebound interpretation. [FIP Rules of Padel 2026](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel-1.pdf) |
| Pickleball | 20 ft × 44 ft (about 6.10 m × 13.41 m), including a 7 ft non-volley zone on each side of the net. [USA Pickleball court construction](https://usapickleball.org/construction/), [official rules](https://usapickleball.org/rules/) |
| Volleyball | 18 m × 9 m. It is a useful cross-sport test for player movement and rally segmentation, but not a reason to infer reliable ball tracking. [FIVB 2025–2028 rules](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN.pdf) |

For partial views, glass reflections, or a camera close to the side line, return `partial_court` or `calibration_uncertain` instead of forcing a homography. For squash and padel, one floor-plane homography cannot model wall rebounds. For table tennis, use tabletop corners and the table plane.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sport recognition:** sample several frames, compare SigLIP2 image/text similarities for sport prompts, and combine that ranking with visible geometry. | **B** for wide views with clear court markings; **C** for close-ups, cropped views, shared gym lines, or visually similar tennis/padel/pickleball courts. | Return top candidates, frame agreement, and a calibrated “needs confirmation” state. | 2 | **Court/table detection and homography:** detect line segments with OpenCV, fit candidate sport templates, score geometric consistency, then map the floor/table plane. CourtAlign is a research reference for tennis and badminton, not proof of phone performance. | Automatic registration **C**; registration after clear user calibration **A/B**. Partial courts and side views are **C**. Squash/padel wall geometry and TT tabletop detection need separate plane handling. | Store selected template, tapped/detected points, reprojection residual, visible-court fraction, and a calibration uncertainty estimate. Abstain on badly conditioned or incomplete geometry. [OpenCV](https://docs.opencv.org/doc/doxygen/html/d2/d48/group__d__projection.html), [CourtAlign](https://github.com/Madou10-lab/CourtAlign) |
| 3 | **Player detection and identity:** pretrained generic person detector plus ByteTrack association. Map each player’s foot point to the court plane. Assign near/far by court side and let the user identify teams and players once. | Singles, fixed camera: **A/B** for presence and court position. Doubles and crossings: **B/C** for persistent identity. Occlusion, camera movement, and players wearing similar clothing cause switches. | Report track IDs as provisional, include observed-frame fraction and identity confidence, and mark long occlusions as unknown. Do not use face identity. Published COCO/MOT scores are not racket-phone identity scores. [YOLOX](https://github.com/Megvii-BaseDetection/YOLOX), [ByteTrack](https://github.com/FoundationVision/ByteTrack) |
| 4 | **Ball or shuttle tracking:** use sport-specific heatmap/object tracking where available, then temporal association and a physics plausibility check. TrackNet-family models and RacketVision are candidate references. | Tennis and badminton **C** on arbitrary phone footage; **B** only when the view, resolution, and ball visibility match a tested domain. TT may reach **B** with stable high-frame-rate footage; **C** at 30 fps or with a hand-held view. Squash, padel, and pickleball are **X/C** because no compatible public weights and amateur-phone benchmark were verified. Volleyball ball tracking remains **C**. | Emit per-frame candidate coordinates, confidence, and missed/occluded spans. Do not interpolate long gaps as observed truth. Shuttle drag and deceleration differ from a ball; bounce, wall contact, and motion blur can break association. | 5 | **Hit/contact and bounce:** combine audio onset from the clip, trajectory change, and racket/wrist proximity. For a bounce, look for a surface-plane event consistent with a trajectory and physics fit. | Hit candidates **B/C** when at least two independent cues agree; otherwise **C**. TT bounce detection improves with high fps and a clear table plane. Badminton has a landing event, not a ball bounce. Squash/padel require wall as well as floor events. | Return an event interval and evidence labels such as `audio_onset`, `track_change`, `pose_near_racket`; do not report an exact contact instant unless observed. Image-space vertical direction alone is not enough to establish a bounce. MonoTrack reports a badminton hit F1 around 0.866 using multiple features, but also reports reprojection error up to 37.1 px in a match. Its code is noncommercial research licensed; weight availability is **UNVERIFIED**. [MonoTrack paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/CVSports/papers/Liu_MonoTrack_Shuttle_Trajectory_Reconstruction_From_Monocular_Badminton_Video_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf), [code](https://github.com/jhwang7628/monotrack) |
| 6 | **Speed:** only claim a court-plane average speed when both observed positions are on the known playing surface: distance between the points divided by timestamp difference. A 3D airborne speed needs a camera model and a physics fit. | Ground-plane speed **B/C** if both endpoints and calibration are strong. Instantaneous serve/smash speed and one-view 3D speed are **C**. Shuttle drag, TT spin/bounce, and squash/padel wall impact each need different models. | Name the metric precisely: `ground_plane_average_speed` or `estimated_3d_speed`. Include value/range, time interval, fps, observed fraction, and assumptions. A homography maps the floor, not an airborne ball. At 30/60/120/240 fps, frame intervals are 33.3/16.7/8.3/4.17 ms; a 30 m/s object would travel 1.0/0.5/0.25/0.125 m between frames. These are arithmetic examples, not model performance. [Badminton study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01412), [tennis study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0263224126009681), [TT study](https://pageperso.univ-lr.fr/laurent.mascarilla/pdf/rfiap2020.pdf) |
| 7 | **In/out:** estimate the bounce/landing point on the correct plane, transform its pixel uncertainty through the inverse homography, and account for line width and object footprint. Use a confidence region, not a single point. | **C** for all sports on ordinary phone video. Camera angle, line visibility, motion blur, bounce timing, and calibration error can move the estimated point by more than the line margin. Squash uses wall lines and tin; TT uses tabletop edges; badminton uses shuttle landing. | Return `in`, `out`, or `too_close_to_call` only if the uncertainty region is wholly on one side of the rule boundary. [ITF ELC evaluation procedures](https://www.itftennis.com/media/15990/elc-evaluation-procedures-v281-jan-2026.pdf) |

Configure sport, rules profile, participants/teams, orientation, and initial server. | Candidate rally boundaries **B/C** when events are visible. | 9 | **Shot classification:** use structured candidate contacts, court zones, player pose, trajectory height/apex, net/wall crossing, and landing zone. A zero-shot VLM on a short clip may be a secondary suggestion, not the ground truth. | **C** across sports. Coarse candidates are more plausible than fine distinctions: serve/return, lob, drop, smash, volley. Spin, deception, intent, and shot quality are not reliably recoverable from one ordinary camera. | Use `shot_candidate` and retain evidence such as trajectory and zone. Do not label it as a confirmed technique. | 10 | **Tactical analytics:** court-normalized player heatmaps, distance, recovery time to a configured base, shot/bounce placement when valid, rally-length distribution, and score-state leverage. | Player-position summaries **A/B** under good calibration and visibility. Ball placement **C** unless tracking is reliable. “Pressure” as score leverage is **B**; mental state and unforced/forced intent are not visually established. | Show observed coverage and missing-track fraction with each heatmap. ## Offline and API implications

A fully offline, whole-match pipeline on a phone is **UNVERIFIED**. There are plausible mobile paths for frame classification and some geometry or person detection, but this review did not verify a complete iOS/Android conversion, memory budget, thermal behavior, or end-to-end processing time for ball tracking across sports. A large local VLM also adds several gigabytes of model storage before runtime memory.

A defensible architecture is:

1. Keep the gallery import, match setup, rule profile, player corrections, score confirmation, and match record on the phone.
2. Run court/player analysis locally only after validating it on the target devices. Store source timestamps and evidence with every result.
3. Make ball, 3D speed, and experimental event analysis optional jobs. For the demo, run them on a prepared development machine or return precomputed results for the pinned demo clips.
4. Expose one versioned match/event schema to the phone app, developer API, SDK, and MCP server. Include `source`, `confidence`, `observed_fraction`, `calibration_id`, and `unknown` states so downstream clients can distinguish a model candidate from a user-confirmed event.
5. Avoid depending on a third-party hosted inference API for the core demo. No provider, price, free-tier limit, or hosted API quota was selected or verified in this track. Any such dependency remains **UNVERIFIED** until checked against its current official terms.

## Recommendation

Build the first release around calibrated court/player movement, an evidence-first replay, rally-length summaries, and deterministic scoring after the player taps who won each point. Treat sport recognition as a setup suggestion. Show ball trajectories and shot labels only as tentative overlays when their evidence is strong. Make line calls and peak speed explicit non-goals for the demo.

Use the licensed, preloaded clips as fixed demo fixtures, with each clip’s source and capture limitations visible in the demo notes. This supports a useful offline-first player experience and a credible API/SDK/MCP story without claiming that the models already solve every sport.

## Three biggest risks

1. **Domain shift:** published model results do not establish reliability on amateur phone video, especially hand-held, low-frame-rate, occluded, or partially framed clips.
2. **Licensing and data rights:** some public datasets permit research or license annotations while leaving source video rights unclear. Do not show dataset footage unless display rights are confirmed.
3. **False certainty:** line calls, speed, shot labels, and automatic scoring can look authoritative even when their inputs are weak. Make uncertainty and user confirmation visible in the product.

- What minimum video contract can the product ask players to meet: fixed phone, full court, 60 fps, and one-time court calibration?
- Which one or two sports must produce the strongest end-to-end demo, and which features can be explicitly marked experimental?
- Will judges supply any clips, and what match formats or scoring variants should the demo support?
